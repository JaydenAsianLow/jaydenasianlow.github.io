02 / About

About me

I love designing things that are good to look at and that people actually enjoy using.

For me a design isn’t finished just because it works. I want it to catch your eye, feel good to use and leave you with a bit of fun, whether that’s an app screen, a poster on a wall or a game you play with your phone.

I sweat the visual details: colour, type, layout and motion. Then I lean on research to make sure the people it’s for find it clear and easy, not just pretty.

I’m studying Human Centered Design & Engineering at the University of Washington. Outside class I design posters for the Skate Club and skate Red Square, which is where the idea for my game came from.

Built with AI

How I got from an idea to a running game

1 · ImagineAn AI image set the look and mood.
2 · PlanI wrote short design docs for the look, the map and the character.
3 · BuildI described each feature to Claude Code, which wrote the game code and Blender scripts.
4 · Play-testI tested every trick on my phone and tuned the feel: tilt dead zone, sensitivity and airtime.

Red Square

Scan a QR code on the laptop, and the phone's browser becomes the controller. No app needed.

1. Press PlayThe laptop shows a QR code.
2. Scan itOn the same Wi-Fi, the controller opens in the phone's browser.
3. Tap StartAllow motion access. The way you hold the phone becomes “level”.
4. SkateTilt and swipes stream to the laptop about 50 times a second.

The controller runs over a secure (HTTPS) connection, because browsers only share tilt sensor data with secure pages.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors

The building footprints and landmarks come from OpenStreetMap, so the layout matches the real campus: Suzzallo, Kane, Odegaard, the brick monoliths, and Rainier Vista leading down to Drumheller Fountain. I modelled the plaza in Blender from photos and lit it for golden hour like the game. Every ledge, bank and stair set is skateable.

Making Me the Skater

Photos → AI turnaround → 3D model → rig → in the game

1 · Reference photos

I photographed my outfit from four sides with my arms out, so the shape of my body and clothes reads clearly.

2 · AI turnaround sheet

An image AI turned the photos into a clean front, side and back sheet: no perspective, flat light and a plain background, which is what a 3D generator needs.

3 · 3D model

An AI 3D generator built the mesh from the sheet. I cleaned it up in Blender and kept it light enough to run in a web browser.

4 · Rig & animation

A standard skeleton lets motion-capture clips drive the model: idle, walking, running, picking up the board and skating.
